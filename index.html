Original heroes.

Accessible hero-based play with meaningful build choices and authored consequences, without requiring players to begin in competitive matchmaking. This is our positioning hypothesis—not a playable experience. Return to the Void remains in concept and pre-production.

Proposal and evidence

Prepare a build, understand its trade-offs, and see an authored choice change what follows. A solo/cooperative learning route is proposed; no new mode or playable hero is demonstrated here.

Versioned schemas and a deterministic fixture exercise an ability, resource cost, cooldown and card modifier. This is a headless rule example, not a playable combat loop, WebGL engine or Unreal demo.

Original heroes and meaningful choices

Build preparation without unnecessary barriers

Can preparing a build stay satisfying without locked counters, unequal gameplay access or excessive learning load? Can abilities explain their impact, failure and counterplay clearly? Player feedback → response → proof

The following comparison frames the requested feedback themes as research questions while source verification is pending. Proposed responses already exist in the original hero-card direction; none is presented as a completed feature.

Build choice and fair access

Feedback to verify: enthusiasm for preparing builds versus objections to locked counters and unequal access.

Proposed response: equal access to gameplay cards during tests, multiple builds and changes at defined safe checkpoints.

Evidence needed: voluntary build-choice tests showing useful alternatives, understandable counters and manageable learning effort.

Readable combat and counterplay

Feedback to verify: differing durability, second-life and counterplay preferences in successor-game discussions—not evidence of defects in Epic asset files.

Proposed response: explicit costs, counters and effect descriptions, with plain-language labels and accessible presentation.

Evidence needed: tutorial comprehension, ability-feedback and failure-understanding tests. These remain research gaps, not assigned new systems.

Story with clear limits

Feedback to verify: whether the intended audience wants tarot-informed stories or a solo/cooperative introduction at all.

Proposed response: authored consequences, no hidden competitive advantages from narrative choices, and understandable play when narrative presentation is disabled.

Evidence needed: observed completion, choice comprehension and return interest. Story/tarot-specific demand and willingness to pay remain unvalidated.

Read the viability assessment and dated source-access register or the existing design proposal. Individual comments and review scores cannot establish market size or product-market fit.

About

Dominique — ibloud

Independent creative technologist running Loptr Lab and Urban Alien Adventures. Working at the intersection of game design, modular engine architecture, and worldbuilding — with a focus on building systems that can be contributed to and extended by a community.

Return to the Void is a working title for a pre-production research project. Its next experiment develops independent characters, tactical card choices and consequential stories. The Diary of Death’s Daughter manga, its matriarch and mythology are Loptr Lab’s creative foundation. Related projects retain their own personalities; this lineage does not insert Rebirth here or merge fictional canon or mechanics. Earlier research remains history. This project does not claim a completed game, rights clearance, or endorsement by another studio.
