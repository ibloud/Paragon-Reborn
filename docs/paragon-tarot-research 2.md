### 1C — LORE CROSS-CONNECTIONS IN MAJOR ARCANA (Design-Relevant)

These relationships should inform visual symbolism and card positioning:

- **Dekker (Fool) ↔ Gideon (Hanged Man):** Dekker discovered the Ashurite temple; Gideon (her crewmate) stole the discovery. Gideon hangs between worlds — literally suspended between his own ambition and Dekker's unrewarded labor.
- **Howitzer (Magician) ↔ Murdock (Emperor):** Murdock arrested Howitzer. Power vs. chaos, law vs. wild potential.
- **Gadget (High Priestess) ↔ TwinBlast (Wheel of Fortune):** Childhood friends; TwinBlast used Gadget's exam project in a Cartel robbery. Her intuition was betrayed by chance.
- **Lt. Belica (Empress) ↔ Murdock (Emperor):** Belica mentored Murdock into law enforcement. Classic Empress/Emperor pairing — authentic to source.
- **Feng Mao (Hierophant) ↔ Sun-Wukong (Staves):** Wukong and Feng Mao have an ongoing philosophical adversarial relationship. Feng Mao trapped Wukong in a cave. These two are cosmically entangled.
- **Muriel / Kallari (Lovers) ↔ Wraith (Cups):** All three are Echelon Program products. Wraith is a third-party mirror — the Lovers are sisters divided by the same program that made Wraith a charming sociopath.
- **Grux (Chariot) ↔ Sevarog (Devil):** Grux was cast out by his gods for waging a brutal war; Sevarog was cast out of Agora entirely and now seeks to destroy the very pantheon that rejected him. Both are god-exiles.
- **Riktor (Justice) ↔ Rampage (Hermit):** Both deal in extreme introspection and extreme consequence. Riktor's justice is external, violent, imposed. Rampage's hermit journey is internal.
- **Greystone (Strength) ↔ Sparrow (Staves):** Sparrow is from the Greystone family — she abandoned the noble life that Greystone represents. His "Strength" is what she rejected.
- **Countess (Death) ↔ Terra (Swords):** Terra's adoptive father was killed by the Countess. This is the most explicit revenge narrative in the deck.
- **Sevarog (Devil) ↔ Serath (Tower):** Serath is the sole survivor of Sevarog's attack. The Tower is the aftermath of the Devil's influence — as it should be in traditional Tarot sequencing.
- **Narbash (Star) ↔ Shinbi (Moon):** Both are exiled musical figures. Narbash uses drumming; Shinbi uses songs that summon wolves. Star/Moon as harmony/illusion, both rooted in music.
- **Steel (Sun) ↔ Yin (Judgement):** Both chose to betray the authority that built them. Steel defied military orders; Yin changed loyalties mid-mission.
- **The Fey (World) ↔ Morigesh (Cups):** The Fey and Morigesh have been at war for centuries over forest territory. The World card carries this tension — expansion consuming everything — and the Cups suit carries Morigesh's counterpoint.
- **Yin (Judgement) ↔ Aurora (Swords):** Best friends from childhood; grew up on the streets of Changdi together. Yin's Judgement is the moment she left Aurora's world behind.
- **Kwang (Swords) ↔ Shinbi (Moon):** Brother and sister. Kwang chased Shinbi to Agora. The sword he carries is for her.
- **Phase (Cups) ↔ Shinbi (Moon):** Phase became Shinbi's underground champion/blogger. A psychic who amplifies another's illusion.
- **Iggy & Scorch (Coins) ↔ Drongo (Staves):** Iggy and Scorch steal from Drongo's scrapyard. Explicit loot/resource conflict across suits — Staves feeds Coins.

### 2B — CHARACTER ROLE ANALYSIS PER SUIT

This section defines the narrative function of each character **before** card assignments are made, so distribution is driven by story logic rather than rotation.

#### SWORDS — Challenges, Conflict, Mental Strength

**Terra — The Avenger / The Goal**
Terra knows exactly what she wants: revenge against the Countess for killing her adoptive father. She is directional, purposeful, driven by grief transformed into will. In the Swords narrative she represents *the reason you fight* — the emotional engine behind the sword's edge. She carries both the wound and the weapon. She is strongest in the high-number cards (6–10) where conflict has become defined and purposeful, and in court cards where agency is fully embodied.

**Kwang — The Duty / The Code**
Kwang is a skilled, dutiful swordsman who completed his quest (finding the Ancestral Sword) only to find it wasn't the end — his sister Shinbi had fled, and duty pivoted from honor to love. He represents *fighting because it's right*, the discipline and moral framework around the sword. He belongs in the low-to-mid numbered cards (Ace through 5) — establishing principles, skill, and the costs of rigid duty — and in court cards of mastery.

**Aurora — The Wound / The Transformation**
Aurora did not choose her power. She sailed into the Frozen North, caught the Freezing Sickness (Everfrost), and miraculously survived, emerging with ice powers as a consequence, not a gift. She represents *what conflict does to you* — the involuntary transformation, the scar that becomes a weapon. She is most present in the difficult middle cards (4–8) where endurance, suffering, and surprising resilience play out, and in certain court cards as a figure of changed nature.

**Narrative Arc of the Swords Suit:**
Kwang establishes the code (early numbers) → Aurora represents the wound that breaks the code (middle cards) → Terra emerges as the one who forges a personal purpose from the wreckage (high cards and courts). The suit tells a story of: *rule → rupture → resolve*.

---

#### STAVES — Action, Initiative, Invention, Energy

**Sun-Wukong — The Spark / The Chaos**
A legend from another universe who transcended time and space. He found Feng Mao to be "a delightful candidate for mischief." Wukong is pure disruptive energy, initiative divorced from consequence, genius without tether. He is *what makes things happen*, the initial spark. Wukong belongs heaviest in the low cards (Ace through 4) — raw fire, explosive potential — and in certain court cards as the wild figure.

**Sparrow — The Mission / The Direction**
Sparrow abandoned a noble Greystone inheritance to seek justice against her apathetic birth family, and formed a new chosen family — a "band of ragtag, like-minded women" for "righteous thievery and merry freedom." She turned energy into purpose, impulse into movement. She represents *action with intention*. Sparrow belongs in the mid-range cards (4–8) where Staves energy becomes a genuine quest, and in court cards of leadership.

**Drongo — The Cost / The Edge**
Drongo was raised by bandits in the Wastelands and operates a scrapyard that everyone else is too afraid to enter. The chemicals and radiation of the Chem Wastes have driven Drongo insane. He represents *what happens when you push into places others won't go* — the reward that comes with a terrible cost, the adventurer who went too far. He belongs in the high-number cards (7–10) where Staves energy exhausts itself or transforms, and in court cards of jaded expertise.

**Narrative Arc of the Staves Suit:**
Wukong ignites (early) → Sparrow channels and directs (middle) → Drongo pays the price and guards the edge (late). The suit tells: *spark → quest → threshold*.

---

#### CUPS — Emotions, Intuition, Relationships

**Morigesh — The Ancient Feeling / The Grudge**
Morigesh is the spirit of the Bathuu Forest, a swamp ancient beyond reckoning, who stole the body of a young sorceress to continue her war against The Fey. Her emotion is the oldest kind — territory, survival, ancient hatred that has outlived its origin. She represents *the feelings we cannot let go of*, the deep emotional current that drives everything. She is strongest in the low-to-mid cards (Ace through 6) establishing the emotional baseline and in cards of intuition, ancient knowing, and powerful empathy.

**Wraith — The Charm / The Hollow**
Wraith is witty, charming, sociopathic. He has a lively social life off-duty and will complete any kill mission without hesitation. He has "ninety-nine reasons for going to Agora, and the truth isn't one." He represents *the emotional mask* — the performance of connection without its core. He is not a villain in the Cups suit; he is the warning about what happens when we use emotional intelligence as a tool rather than a truth. He belongs in cards of emotional complexity, manipulation, social navigation, and the dark side of relationship (mid-range, 5–9).

**Phase — The Hidden Depth / The Amplifier**
Phase has psychic powers. She was sent to Tryon Industries for "special care," escaped, hid in the shadows of Omeda City, and went online as an underground music blogger — ultimately bringing Shinbi her viral fame. She feels everything other people feel, amplifies it, and redirects it without ever quite being seen. She represents *empathy as a superpower and a burden*, connection without visibility. She belongs in the high-number cards and court cards, especially those involving intuition, hidden knowledge, support from unexpected places, and emotional overflow.

**Narrative Arc of the Cups Suit:**
Morigesh establishes the depth of feeling (early, ancient, unyielding) → Wraith introduces emotional complexity and disconnection (middle, charming and hollow) → Phase resolves by feeling everything, amplifying it for others, never seeking the spotlight (late, psychic overflow). The suit tells: *ancient feeling → performed feeling → transcended feeling*.

---

#### COINS — Work, Finances, Long-Term Goals, Material World

**Iggy & Scorch — The Partnership / The Scrappy Start**
Cast out as runts — Iggy from his goblin tribe, Scorch caught in a trap — they became inseparable because they had nothing and chose each other. They steal from Drongo's scrapyard together. They represent *the foundation of material security built from nothing*, resourcefulness, loyalty as a survival strategy, and the joy of small accumulations. They belong in the low-to-mid cards (Ace through 6), establishing the Coins suit's theme of building from scratch, and in cards of partnership, small gains, and creative scavenging.

**Revenant — The Dual Nature / The Negotiated Self**
Jedidiah Sparks was the most feared bounty hunter on Omeda until an ancient spirit possessed him in Letha. After struggling for control of their shared body, Jedidiah and the spirit "reached an uneasy truce" and now exist as Revenant. He represents *the merger of worldly drive and something beyond*, material ambition complicated by an internal other. He belongs in the mid-range cards (4–8) where Coins energy involves negotiation, compromise, holding two things at once — the tension between immediate reward and longer obligation.

**Grim.exe — The Transformation / The Weapon**
Grim stowed away to Omeda City, was picked up by the Ying Mei Cartel as a mascot, won EXE in a bone dice game, and "everything changed — he was no longer a mascot of the criminal underworld, but a killing machine." He is the rags-to-power story in its most extreme form. He represents *what happens to material ambition when resources become overwhelming*, when the thing you won changes who you are. He belongs in the high-number cards (7–10) and court cards involving legacy, power, mastery of resources, and the cost of becoming.

**Narrative Arc of the Coins Suit:**
Iggy & Scorch build from nothing together (early) → Revenant negotiates a complicated dual existence (middle) → Grim.exe transforms entirely by what he has accumulated (late). The suit tells: *scrappy start → difficult negotiation → complete transformation by wealth/power*.

## SWORDS SUIT (14 cards)
**Suit Theme:** Challenges, conflict, mental fortitude, the costs of conflict
**Character Roles:**
- **Kwang** — Duty, skill, the code (primary: Ace–5, King)
- **Aurora** — Wound, transformation, involuntary power (primary: 4–8, Queen)
- **Terra** — Resolve, revenge, purposeful will (primary: 6–10, Page, Knight)
**Distribution Logic:** Kwang opens with principle → Aurora carries the suffering of the middle → Terra closes with driven purpose. Court cards: Page=Terra's origin, Knight=Terra's mission, Queen=Aurora's changed nature, King=Kwang's mastery.

### ACE OF SWORDS
**Character:** Kwang
**Secondary:** None (Ace = pure essence of suit)
**Traditional Correspondence:** Pure mental clarity; breakthrough; truth cutting through confusion; the sword of the mind offered, not yet wielded
**Upright Meaning:** A decisive new beginning; clarity cuts through doubt; the moment before action where truth is most visible; a challenge accepted
**Reversed Meaning:** Confusion mistaken for clarity; a decision made too quickly; the mind cutting itself
**Paragon Interpretation:** Kwang's hand closes on the Ancestral Sword for the first time — the culmination of a quest, and simultaneously the start of a new one. This is the moment he understands his duty completely and chooses it.
**Visual Scene:** Close on Kwang's gauntleted hands lifting the Ancestral Sword upright from a stone or altar. A single ray of light from above catches the blade. His face is calm, resolute.
**Symbolic Objects:** The Ancestral Sword (upright, glowing faintly); stone altar or reliquary
**Environment:** An ancient stone vault or mountain shrine — cold, still, sacred
**Suggested Pose/Action:** Solemn two-handed lift, sword vertical, tip toward the sky
**Relationship to Suit:** This is the seed from which all 13 other Swords cards grow. Kwang's pure ideal — duty fulfilled — is what the rest of the suit will test, wound, and eventually answer.

---

### TWO OF SWORDS
**Character:** Kwang
**Secondary:** None
**Traditional Correspondence:** Stalemate; a difficult choice avoided; blindfolded reason; peace purchased at the cost of decision
**Upright Meaning:** A truce that cannot hold; refusing to choose; holding two competing loyalties at once; the impasse before action
**Reversed Meaning:** A forced decision with incomplete information; blindfold removed too soon; betrayal of a hard-won peace
**Paragon Interpretation:** Kwang completed his duty (the Ancestral Sword) only to find his sister Shinbi had fled. His code says one thing; his heart says another. He has not moved yet.
**Visual Scene:** Kwang stands at a crossroads with both arms extended, a sword in each hand, tips touching across a ravine or threshold. His eyes are downcast or averted. Behind him: the direction of honor. Before him: the direction of family.
**Symbolic Objects:** Two swords held parallel; a ravine or border; the moon behind him (connecting to Shinbi's card)
**Environment:** A mountain ridge at dusk; two paths visible
**Suggested Pose/Action:** Still, balanced, arms outstretched — the posture of perfect hesitation
**Relationship to Suit:** The Ace gave the sword; the Two shows the first test — what happens when duty points one way and love another.

---

### THREE OF SWORDS
**Character:** Aurora
**Secondary:** None
**Traditional Correspondence:** Heartbreak; grief; a painful truth; the wound that cannot be unseen
**Upright Meaning:** A significant loss; pain that must be acknowledged before healing; truth that hurts; an unavoidable sorrow
**Reversed Meaning:** Wallowing in grief; refusing to process pain; the sorrow that has become identity
**Paragon Interpretation:** Aurora did not choose her power. She caught Everfrost and it nearly killed her. The Three of Swords is that moment — the sickness reaching its worst before the miraculous survival.
**Visual Scene:** Aurora collapsed or suspended in ice water, three frost-edged sword shapes visible as cracks in the ice around her. Her expression is still — not dead, but at the threshold.
**Symbolic Objects:** Ice shards shaped like swords surrounding her; frozen water; a faint pulse of frost-blue light from her core
**Environment:** The Frozen North; beneath the ice surface, looking up
**Suggested Pose/Action:** Suspended, half-prone, the body in the moment of maximum vulnerability before the turning point
**Relationship to Suit:** This is the wound that transforms Aurora and gives the Swords suit its central cost. Without this card, Aurora has no reason to become what she becomes.

---

### FOUR OF SWORDS
**Character:** Aurora
**Secondary:** None
**Traditional Correspondence:** Rest after conflict; recovery; meditation; a pause before the next battle; contemplation
**Upright Meaning:** A necessary stillness; healing after a wound; removing yourself from conflict to gather strength; strategic withdrawal
**Reversed Meaning:** Forced inactivity; refusing to rest; the wound that will not close because it is not given time
**Paragon Interpretation:** Aurora survived Everfrost. Now she rests — or rather, holds still while her new power settles into her. She is different. She is learning what she has become.
**Visual Scene:** Aurora seated or reclined in a cave of ice of her own making — still, eyes closed, ice patterns forming around her like frozen breath. Peaceful but transformed.
**Symbolic Objects:** Ice crystals in geometric patterns radiating from her hands; a still surface; one sword laid flat before her, three mounted on the wall
**Environment:** An ice cave; warm light from an ambiguous source (she is generating it)
**Suggested Pose/Action:** Resting, hands loose, frost-breath visible, face calm
**Relationship to Suit:** Follows the wound (Three) with recovery. Aurora is integrating her new nature. This is the inhale before the exhale.

---

### FIVE OF SWORDS
**Character:** Aurora
**Secondary:** None
**Traditional Correspondence:** Hollow victory; conflict for its own sake; taking more than needed; the winner who loses anyway
**Upright Meaning:** A battle won at too high a cost; pride before wisdom; using power to defeat instead of to protect; enemies turned or fled
**Reversed Meaning:** Walking away from a fight; releasing a conflict that only you are still in; admitting the victory wasn't worth it
**Paragon Interpretation:** Aurora's new ice power is overwhelming. She can freeze enemies solid. But her childhood friend Yin is now on a different path. Some conflicts cannot be won without losing someone.
**Visual Scene:** Aurora stands in the foreground, ice still swirling from a recent battle. Several fallen figures or retreating shadows behind her. Her expression is not triumphant — it is ambivalent. Swords scattered on frozen ground.
**Symbolic Objects:** Scattered or broken swords; retreating figures; the ice itself (her power) used as the tool of victory; cold light
**Environment:** A battlefield frozen mid-scene; Changdi streets or a cold open plain
**Suggested Pose/Action:** Standing, sword lowered, surveying a scene that should feel like victory but doesn't
**Relationship to Suit:** The transformation (Three, Four) has given Aurora power. The Five tests whether she uses it wisely. She hasn't. Yet.

---

### SIX OF SWORDS
**Character:** Terra
**Secondary:** (Optional background: Kwang or Aurora, representing departure from what was)
**Traditional Correspondence:** Transition; moving away from turbulence toward calmer waters; necessary passage; guided travel through difficulty
**Upright Meaning:** Movement away from a painful situation; a guide helping you cross to safer ground; the worst is behind; transition requires letting go of the shore
**Reversed Meaning:** Resistance to necessary change; clinging to a painful situation; the journey taken too soon or in the wrong direction
**Paragon Interpretation:** Terra leaves Kilgar for the first time. Her adoptive father is dead. The only direction forward is toward the Countess. She boards or mounts, carrying everything she has, heading into unknown territory.
**Visual Scene:** Terra on a boat, raft, or mount crossing open water or a wide plain. Six swords are planted upright in the prow or carried on her back. Her face is set — not grieving, not hopeful, just going.
**Symbolic Objects:** Six upright swords; a vessel in motion; receding shore; open horizon ahead
**Environment:** Open water, river crossing, or wide road at dusk — between worlds
**Suggested Pose/Action:** Seated or riding forward, facing the direction of travel, back to the viewer or profile
**Relationship to Suit:** Terra's first appearance in the numbered cards. The Six of Swords is the moment she commits. Kwang and Aurora's stories gave context for why this journey is hard; Terra now begins hers.

---

### SEVEN OF SWORDS
**Character:** Terra
**Secondary:** None
**Traditional Correspondence:** Strategy over brute force; cunning; taking what you can when you cannot take all; deception in service of survival
**Upright Meaning:** A clever approach to an impossible situation; resourcefulness; taking the unconventional path; not everything has to be a straight fight
**Reversed Meaning:** Being caught in deception; the clever plan that fails; exposing yourself through overconfidence
**Paragon Interpretation:** Terra cannot fight the Countess directly — not yet. She gathers what she needs: armor from across the land, intelligence about the Countess's movements. She is not yet the hero she intends to be, so she is strategic.
**Visual Scene:** Terra carrying five swords in her arms, glancing back over her shoulder — two swords left behind, visible in the distance. She is collecting what she needs without raising an alarm. Her expression is focused, not guilty.
**Symbolic Objects:** Swords being gathered; a partially lit environment; Terra's collected armor (a detail of the "best armor from across the land" she is known for assembling)
**Environment:** Armory, battlefield aftermath, or a city market at night
**Suggested Pose/Action:** Moving with purpose, arms laden with swords, one glance back — the look of someone calculating, not fleeing
**Relationship to Suit:** Terra learns the indirect path. This is necessary humility before the direct confrontation of the Eight through Ten.

---

### EIGHT OF SWORDS
**Character:** Aurora
**Secondary:** None
**Traditional Correspondence:** Self-imposed restriction; feeling trapped by circumstances that may be illusions; fear keeping you still; victim thinking
**Upright Meaning:** A situation that feels inescapable but isn't; the trap is partly self-made; fear and mental loops; waiting to be freed rather than freeing yourself
**Reversed Meaning:** Removing the blindfold; recognizing the bars are imaginary; breaking free with difficulty; or conversely, refusing to see the restriction at all
**Paragon Interpretation:** Aurora's ice power can trap others — but it also traps her. Her best friend Yin is on a different side now. Aurora cannot fight Yin; she also cannot let Yin continue. She is frozen in her own conflict.
**Visual Scene:** Aurora standing very still, surrounded by eight swords planted in ice around her — she has made this cage herself. Her eyes are open but unfocused. The ice binds without touching her.
**Symbolic Objects:** Eight swords in ice forming a loose circle; Aurora at the center; ice that she generated; the pattern looks like a frozen moment
**Environment:** A frozen clearing — her environment is her own creation
**Suggested Pose/Action:** Standing, arms slightly out, swords surrounding but not touching her — the posture of someone who could move but hasn't
**Relationship to Suit:** Aurora's final major appearance in the numbered cards. Her wound (Three) and transformation (Four) led to power (Five) which led to this — the moment she recognizes that power without direction is its own prison.

### NINE OF SWORDS
**Character:** Terra

The Paragon character assets (39 heroes total) were released by Epic Games as free downloads from the Unreal Engine Marketplace. The following conditions apply:

- Assets are free of charge
- Licensed **for use in Unreal Engine 4 projects only** (3D assets/animation Blueprints)
- The license terms cover UE4 development use — **not commercial printing of physical cards**
- For a physical tarot deck, the artwork will need to be **original illustration** based on the character designs, not direct exports of the UE4 assets

**This means:** The character designs, names, and Paragon IP are owned by Epic Games. Any commercial product using the characters must either:
1. Obtain explicit licensing from Epic Games for commercial merchandise use, OR
2. Confirm that the deck is non-commercial (fan project), in which case fan-use norms apply but no formal protection exists, OR
3. Commission original artwork heavily inspired by but not copying the 3D model assets directly

**Recommendation for handoff:** The design system should treat all 78 cards as requiring original illustration briefs (which this document provides in the Visual Scene / Pose fields). The Paragon character designs serve as visual reference only.
